## Observed problem and unchanged acceptance

All three current background cases failed (exit 1, zero passes) because the Sidebar expand button intersects the rectangle of a containing layout element. Report `/private/tmp/meetily-theme-background-current.json` records this geometry failure, not a detected near-white color. Large containers contain descendants with different paints: demanding one uniform rendered background for their entire rectangle is inappropriate for proving absence of light surfaces. The exact text/focus resolver is still appropriate for its small measured regions and stays unchanged.

Success criterion 6 remains: actual Home, Settings and Meeting Details have no near-white app background island in dark mode, plus no horizontal overflow. No transient semantic probe can satisfy it. A background is near-white when all RGB channels are at least 245, as in the existing suite. The new proof certifies three separate RGB-channel upper bounds across the complete main rectangle, including overlapping and translucent background layers. In every spatial cell at least one channel bound must be below 245; this proves the existing all-channels-near-white condition cannot occur there. Opaque saturated yellow/red backgrounds must not be rejected merely because their brightest channel exceeds 245. An inconclusive bound fails rather than passing. Retain the existing deliberate checked-switch background exception, explicitly report its nodes; do not add another exception. Text, meaningful icon/indicator foregrounds and borders are separate acceptance subjects, not background layers. A white box-shadow remains background paint and cannot use that switch exception.

## Conservative paint inventory

Require one `main` and an explicit opaque HTML canvas with root opacity 1. Start the three bounds at that canvas RGB. Walk every DOM element, including zero-size nodes and nodes whose principal border box is outside main. Inspect box-shadow and generated pseudo backgrounds/images/shadows independently, before deciding whether the principal background fill or replaced content has a positive-area rectangle intersecting main. A zero-size or off-scope owner may still paint a visible spread-shadow or positioned pseudo inside main. Only the principal fill/replaced content can use that area/intersection filter, after unsupported effects that could enlarge its paint have been rejected. Do not filter on background alpha or on a minimum area. Include offscreen parts of main; do not limit coverage to the visible screenshot. Establish non-painting through the complete display-none/zero-opacity ancestor chain where applicable; do not assume visibility-hidden ancestors hide a visibility-visible descendant. Use DOM bounding rectangles as conservative supersets; do not discard paint because of rounded corners or clipping.

For each solid background, retain its RGB components, alpha, conservative rectangle and product of node/ancestor opacities. Transparent backgrounds contribute zero, but their other paint properties still need validation. Handle a background fill and its image as separate potential layers. Support only ordinary `linear-gradient` with a direction/angle and computed sRGB `rgb`/`rgba` color stops (optional simple percentage stops). Reject every URL/image, radial/conic/repeating gradient, multiple images, interpolation-space modifier, unknown color syntax or other unsupported grammar. For a supported gradient, maximum of each separate source channel and maximum stop alpha over all stops are upper bounds; treating these independent maxima as one uniform layer over its full rectangle overestimates the actual paint and is safe. No gradient becomes accepted for text/focus contrast.

Parse every box-shadow color/layer, including inset: its per-channel source components and effective alpha are bounds. Treat its extent as the whole main rectangle, avoiding a finite-blur cutoff or an assumed shadow rasterizer. This may be inconclusive for bright shadows, which must fail. Dark shadows cannot increase the bound. An ordinary outer shadow with all offsets/blur/spread exactly zero paints no area outside the border box; a narrowly proven empty-shadow case may contribute zero, with a regression for a transparent-background element. Do not generalize this to nonzero spreads, offsets, blur or inset paint. Validate pseudo-element background/image/shadow paint too; if its geometry cannot be established, conservatively cover the whole main rectangle. Generated foreground text is not a background fill. Replaced media/canvas/iframe or unsupported pseudo paint must fail, not be silently omitted.

Reject non-normal blending, filters/backdrop filters, masks/clip-path ambiguity, unsupported transforms/zoom, non-finite values and any unsupported effect. Preserve the existing translation-only coordinate contract. Position/z-index do not themselves require rejection: the bound deliberately considers every possible order of the supported layers.

## Bound calculation and soundness

Split the main rectangle at every collected background rectangle boundary, clipped to the scope. Each resulting open rectangular cell has a constant set of possible background layers. Also cover boundaries by conservative closed rectangle intersection; never certify a cell while omitting a boundary-only layer. Use conservative inclusion where an effect's geometry is unknown.

For each cell and each RGB channel separately, let `u` be that channel upper bound. A possible layer with source component `c` and effective alpha `a` can only raise it to `u + max(0, c - u) * a`; dropping layers that would darken that channel is conservative. Process source components in ascending order independently per channel to maximize its result across possible stacking orders. The three channel bounds may come from different hypothetical orders; this is conservative, not a claim of one exact rendered RGB color. This deliberately overestimates independent child contributions inside opacity groups: treating each contribution separately at the product opacity does not create a lower bound. Explain/prove this group-opacity property in the implementation review, including nested groups and an external sibling layer. One proof models each alpha/opacity as a Bernoulli gate: descendants share ancestor gates, while independent per-layer gates with the same marginal product opacity overestimate the expected maximum active component for the selected channel. Inactive-layer events are monotone decreasing and positively associated, so their joint probability is at least the product of their marginals; ascending composition computes the independent expected maximum. Require the implementation to respect this bound model and its restrictions. If this property cannot be established, do not implement the simplification: use a group-aware bound instead.

Use full floating-point values with an explicit upward error margin per operation; define finite caps for nodes, layers, cells and operations so total rounding error stays well below the margin. Exceeding a cap is unresolved/FAIL. Never round the comparison downward, use a percentile, sample sparse points or mask pixels. A cell with all three bounds at/above 245, or any unsupported/inconclusive scene, fails the route check. Record each cell’s channel bounds and aggregate `max(min(uR,uG,uB))` with its location/contributing layers to distinguish a real light island from a conservative failure. Every actual channel is bounded by its corresponding `u`; at least one bound below 245 in every cell proves absence of the original all-channels-near-white island criterion.
